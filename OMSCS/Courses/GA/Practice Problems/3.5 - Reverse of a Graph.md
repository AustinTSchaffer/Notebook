# 3.5 - Reverse of a Graph
![[Pasted image 20260223151143.png]]

## Algorithm
We first construct an edgeless graph $G^R$, copying the nodes from $G$, but not the edges. Then for each of the edges in G, we reverse the edge and add it to $G^R$.

## Justification of Correctness
The edges of a graph connect only 2 nodes. In order to reverse a single directed edge in a graph, you must simply change the source and sink node for that edge. By projecting all of the edges of $G$ into $G^R$ such that they all are now pointing in the opposite direction ensures that $G^R$ is the reverse of $G$. None of the edges of $G$ will be missed during this operation. None of the edges of $G^R$ will be duplicated, because the source edges were also not duplicated. Given that $G$ is assumed to be a simple graph, there are no two edges in $G$ such that $e_1=e_2$. Reversing $e_1$ and $e_2$ has the same inequality.

By first copying the nodes from $G$ to a new graph $G^R$, we ensures that isolated vertices are included in the graph, and that all vertices in the adjacency list are pre-allocated.

## Runtime Analysis
Constructing $G^R$ from the nodes of $G$ runs in $O(n)$ time. Iterating over the edges of $G$ runs in $O(n+m)$ time. For each edge, adding the edge to $G^R$ is a constant time operation ($O(1)$).

- It's a constant-time operation to lookup the vertex $v$ in $G^R$
- It's a constant-time operation to append the vertex $u$ to the adjacency list for $v$.

Iterating over the edges of $G$ dominates the other operation, therefore the overall runtime for this algorithm is $O(n+m)$.

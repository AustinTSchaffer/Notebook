# 7.99.1 - Feasibility and Boundedness
Check whether this LP is feasible/unbounded using the processes from [[07.3 - LP - Geometry]] and [[07.4 - LP - Duality]].

- $\max \space -3x_1+x_2$
- s.t.
	- $x_1-2x_2 \le -2$
	- $-x_1 \le -2$
	- $-x_1+x_2 \le 8$
	- $x_1,x_2 \ge 0$

Geometrically, this one appears to be feasible and bounded.

## Primal LP
We need to show that this LP is feasible. This can be accomplished by finding any pair of points which satisfies the constraints. We can generate all possible vertices of the feasible region through converting the inequalities in the constraints to equalities, and then joining enough of them to solve for the variables ($x_1, x_2$). We then need to check that point to see if it lies in the feasible region by comparing it against all constraints.

For this particular problem, there are 5 total constraints, including the non-negativity constraints. There are also 2 variables. Therefore, we need to combine all pairs of the 5 equations to produce ${5 \choose 2}=10$ systems of equations, which will generate 0 to 10 possible vertices of the feasible region. For each system of equations:

- It's possible that at least 2 of the equations are parallel in at least one dimension. As a result, they will not intersect at a single point. These systems do not produce a point, and therefore do not describe a vertex of the feasible region.
	- In this example, $x_1=0$ and $-x_1=-2$ are parallel. There is no solution to this system, and therefore no vertex is produced.
	- In DPV 7.12 "Prove Optimality of Point", we have a 3-variable LP, requiring that we solve ${5 \choose 3} = 10$ systems of equations. 2 of these systems do not produce point solutions.
- If all of the equations in the system intersect at a single point, then we've found a *possible* vertex of the feasible region. We then need to check those points against every constraint to see if the point is a valid solution to the LP. If so, then it forms a vertex of the feasible region.

If we do this process for this , we end up with $(x_1=2,x_2=2)$ and $(x_1=2,x_2=10)$ as the vertices of the feasible region. Vertex $(x_1=2,x_2=10)$ maximizes the objective function with a value of $4$.

### "Show your work"
Here are the equations, numbered to make it easier 

1. $x_1-2x_2 = -2$
2. $-x_1 = -2$
3. $-x_1+x_2=8$
4. $x_1=0$
5. $x_2=0$

#### EQs 1 and 2
Equations 1 and 2 in isolation give us a linear system of equations.
- $x_1-2x_2 = -2$
- $-x_1 = -2$
- These give us:
	- $x_1=2$
	- $2-2x_2=-2$
	- $-2x_2=-4$
	- $x_2=2$

$(x_1=2,x_2=2)$ satisfies all of the constraints of the LP, meaning the LP's feasible region is not empty. There are 2 other pairs of equations we can check.

#### EQs 2 and 3
Equations 2 and 3 in isolation.
- $-x_1 = -2$
- $-x_1+x_2 = 8$
- These give us:
	- $x_1=2$
	- $-2+x_2=8$
	- $x_2=10$

$(x_1=2,x_2=10)$ also satisfies all the constraints of the LP.

#### EQs 1 and 3
Equations 1 and 3 in isolation.
- $x_1-2x_2 = -2$
- $-x_1+x_2=8$
- These give us:
	- $x_1=-2+2x_2$
	- $-(-2+2x_2)+x_2=8$
	- $2-2x_2+x_2=8$
	- $-2x_2+x_2=6$
	- $-x_2=6$
	- $x_2=-6$
	- $x_1-2(-6)=-2$
	- $x_1+12=-2$
	- $x_1=-14$

$(x_1=-14,x_2=-6)$ does **not** satisfy the constraints, and does not form a vertex of the feasible region.

#### The Rest...
Combining EQs 4 and/or 5 with any of the other equations do no result in any points within the feasible region. We can exhaustively work these out, but thankfully we can graph the feasible region in 2D, which shows that the feasible region does not intersect with the x-axis nor the y-axis. See the graph below.

In the case where we can't easily graph the feasible region (e.g. 3D+, other complications), we can just fall back on performing the remaining combinations of equations, producing a total of ${5 \choose 2} = 10$ possible vertices of the feasible region.

1. EQs 1 and 2 $\rightarrow(x_1=2,x_2=2)$ 
2. EQs 1 and 3 $\rightarrow (x_1=-14,x_2=-6)$ (not in feasible region)
3. EQs 1 and 4 $\rightarrow \space ...$ (not in feasible region, geometrically)
4. EQs 1 and 5 $\rightarrow \space ...$ (not in feasible region, geometrically)
5. EQs 2 and 3 $\rightarrow (x_1=2,x_2=10)$
6. EQs 2 and 4 $\rightarrow$ (no solution, equations do not intersect)
7. EQs 2 and 5 $\rightarrow \space ...$ (not in feasible region, geometrically)
8. EQs 3 and 4 $\rightarrow \space ...$ (not in feasible region, geometrically)
9. EQs 3 and 5 $\rightarrow \space ...$ (not in feasible region, geometrically)
10. EQs 4 and 5 $\rightarrow (x_1=0, x_2=0)$ (trivial point) (not in feasible region)

## Dual LP
Up to this point, we've numerically determined the vertices of the Primal LP's feasible region. The feasible region only has 2 vertices. From this, we can infer that **the feasible region of the Primal LP is unbounded**, since we can't form a closed shape with 2 vertices. However, even if we found a 100 vertex feasible region for the primal LP, we wouldn't know whether that feasible region was bounded unless we either (a) look at it geometrically or (b) check whether the Dual LP is feasible.

We first need to identify the Dual LP.

- **Primal**
	- $\max \space -3x_1+x_2$
	- s.t.
		- $x_1-2x_2 \le -2$
		- $-x_1 \le -2$
		- $-x_1+x_2 \le 8$
		- $x_1,x_2 \ge 0$
- **Dual**
	- $\min \space -2y_1 - 2y_2 + 8y_3$
	- s.t.
		- $y_1-y_2-y_3 \ge -3$
		- $-2y_1 + y_3 \ge 1$
		- $y_1,y_2,y_3 \ge 0$

To prove that the Primal LP is bounded, we need to check to make sure that the Dual LP is feasible. This can be accomplished by finding **ANY** valid vertex for the feasible region. We can also prove that the point $(x_1=2,x_2=10) \rightarrow 4$ is optimal for the Primal LP by finding the optimal solution to the Dual LP. For this question, we only care that the LP is unbounded, so we can commit to doing less work.

Let's review just these 3 inequalities.
- $y_1-y_2-y_3 \ge -3$
- $-2y_1 + y_3 \ge 1$
- $y_2 \ge 0$

Converting them to equations:
- $y_1-y_2-y_3 = -3$
- $-2y_1 + y_3 = 1$
- $y_2=0$

Solving $y_1$ in terms of $y_3$
- $y_1-y_2-y_3=-3$
- $y_1 - 0 - y_3=-3$
- $y_1=-3+y_3$

Solving $y_3$
- $-2y_1+y_3 = 1$
- $-2(-3+y_3)+y_3=1$
- $6-2y_3+y_3=1$
- $6-y_3=1$
- $-y_3=-5$
- $y_3=5$

Solving $y_1$
- $y_1-y_2-y_3=-3$
- $y_1 - 0 - 5 = -3$
- $y_1 = 2$

Solution $(y_1=2, y_2=0, y_3=5)$ satisfies all constraints of the Dual LP, and therefore the Dual LP is feasible, which means the Primal LP is **bounded**.

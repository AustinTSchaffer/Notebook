### Upper Bound
- Let $y=(y_1,y_2,y_3,y_4)=(0,1/3,1,8/3)$
- Let's number the constraints
	1. $x_1 \le 300$
	2. $x_2 \le 200$
	3. $x_1+3x_2+2x_3 \le 1000$
	4. $x_2+3x_3 \le 500$
- We know that any potential solution satisfies all 4 of these constraints. We also know that any potential solution satisfies any linear combination of these constraints.
- Take the linear combination defined by $y$
	- $y_1c_1 + y_2c_2 + y_3c_3 + y_4c_4$
	- $y_1(x_1) + y_2(x_2) + y_3(x_1+3x_2+2x_3) + y_4(x_2+3x_3)$
		- $\le y_1(300)+y_2(200)+y_3(1000)+y_4(500)$
	- Any feasible point $x$ satisfies this new inequality for any non-negative $y$
	- Before we substitute our given $y$, we can substitute and combine like terms.
	- $x_1(y_1+y_3)+x_2(y_2+3y_3+y_4)+x_3(2y_3+3y_4)$
		- $\le y_1(300)+y_2(200)+y_3(1000)+y_4(500)$
	- Plugging in $y$ gives us this: $x_1+6x_2+10x_3 \le 2400$

## Dual LP
- How did we find $y$?
- We're trying to find an upper bound on the objective function, so we need our values for $y$ to be at least the same as the coefficients in the objective function.
- Let's ignore that specific $y$, what condition do we need?
	- Remember this:
	- $x_1(y_1+y_3)+x_2(y_2+3y_3+y_4)+x_3(2y_3+3y_4)$
		- $\le y_1(300)+y_2(200)+y_3(1000)+y_4(500)$
	- The top looks like our objective function. The bottom looks like our current best profit projection.
- We can set the $y$ values s.t. they're larger than the objective coefficients.
	- $y_1+y_3 \ge 1$
	- $y_2+3y_3+y_4 \ge 6$
	- $2y_3 + 3y_4 \ge 10$
- Any $y$ satisfying these 3 constraints
	- The LHS will be at least as large as our objective function.
	- The RHS will serve as an upper bound on the LHS, and therefore is an upper bound on our objective function.
	- We want the smallest possible upper bound.
	- Therefore we need to minimize $300y_1 + 200y_2 + 1000y_3 + 500y_4$
	- This is the **Dual LP**
- **Dual LP Construction**
	- Take an LP in standard form.
	- Number the non-trivial constraints in the original LP.
	- Create $y=[y_1,...,y_m]$ where $m$ is the number of constraints in the original LP. Associate each $y_j$ with a constraint.
	- Some terminology to refer to the original LP
		- $n$ is the number of variables/dimensions in the original LP
		- $m$ is the number of constraints in the original LP, not including the $x\ge 0$ constraint(s).
		- $c_{a,obj}$ is the coefficient for variable $a$ in the objective function
		- $c_{ab}$ is the coefficient for variable $a$ in constraint $b$
		- $b_{b}$ is the upper-bound applied to constraint $b$
	- For each variable $x_i$ in the original LP
		- Take the coefficients $c=[c_{i1},...,c_{im}]$ of $x_i$ from each constraint.
		- Generate a new constraint using $y$
		- This new constraint will be: $(c_{i1}y_1+c_{i2}y_2+...+c_{im}y_m)$
		- Take $x_i$'s coefficient from the objective function ($c_{i,obj})$. That becomes the lower-bound on this new constraint.
		- This leaves us with: $c_{i1}y_1+c_{i2}y_2+...+c_{im}y_m \ge c_{i,obj}$
	- We define a new objective function based on $y$ the upper-bounds of constraints in the original LP. We aim to minimize this objective function.
		- $\min \space b_1y_1+b_2y_2+...+b_my_m$
	- In the Dual LP
		- There will be $m$ variables
		- There will be $n$ constraints

## General Form
- Primal LP
	- $n$ variables
	- $m$ constraints
	- $\max \space c^Tx$
	- $Ax \le b$
	- $x \ge 0$
- Dual LP
	- $m$ variables
	- $n$ constraints
	- $\min \space b^Ty$
	- $A^Ty \ge c$
	- $y \ge 0$

## Dual of Dual
> The Dual of the Dual is the Primal

In order to make this process work, we have to transform the dual into "standard form" by converting the min to a max, and flipping the inequality.

## Example 2
### Original
- Objective: $\max \space 5x_1-7x_2+2x_3$
- Constraints:
	- $x_1+x_2-4x_3 \le 1$
	- $2x_1-x_2 \ge 3$
	- $x_1,x_2,x_3 \ge 0$

### Primal LP in Standard Form
- Objective: $\max \space 5x_1-7x_2+2x_3$
- Constraints:
	- $x_1+x_2-4x_3 \le 1$
	- $-2x_1+x_2 \le -3$ (this needed flipping)
	- $x_1,x_2,x_3 \ge 0$

### Primal LP in Matrix Form
- $\max \space c^Tx$
- $Ax \le b$

$$c=\begin{bmatrix}5 \\ -7 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}$$
$$x=\begin{bmatrix}x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3\end{bmatrix}$$
$$
A=\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 1 & -4 \\
-2 & 1 & 0
\end{bmatrix}
$$
$$b=\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix}$$

$$\max\left(c^Tx\right) \implies \max \left(\begin{bmatrix}5 & -7 & 2\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3\end{bmatrix}\right)$$
$$Ax \le b \implies \begin{bmatrix}
1 & 1 & -4 \\
-2 & 1 & 0
\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3\end{bmatrix} \le \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix}$$

### Dual LP in Matrix Form
- $\min \space b^Ty$
- $A^Ty \ge c$

$$b^T=\begin{bmatrix}1 & -3\end{bmatrix}$$
$$y=\begin{bmatrix}y_1 \\ y_2 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$A^T=\begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 \\ 1 & 1 \\ -4 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$c=\begin{bmatrix}5 \\ -7 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}$$

### Dual LP as Inequalities
- Objective: $\min \space y_1 - 3y_2$
- Constraints:
	- $y_1-2y_2 \ge 5$
	- $y_1+y_2 \ge -7$
	- $-4y_1 \ge 2$

## Weak Duality
**Theorem:**
- Feasible $x$ for primal LP
- Feasible $y$ for dual LP
- $c^Tx\le b^Ty$
- $y$ gives an upper bound on the objective function for the primal LP
- LHS is the Primal LP objective function
- RHS is the Dual LP objective function

**Corollary 1:** If we find feasible $x$ and feasible $y$ where $c^Tx = b^Ty$, then $x$ and $y$ are both optimal.

**Corollary 2:**
- If the **primal** LP is **unbounded**, then the **dual** LP is **infeasible**.
	- (The RHS will have to be larger than infinity, which is not possible.)
- If the **dual** LP is **unbounded**, then the **primal** LP is **infeasible**.
	- (The LHS will have to be smaller than negative infinity, which is undefined.)
- The implications here only go in one direction. Both LPs can be infeasible.

## Check Unbounded
We can check the primal to see if its feasible. This is fairly straightforward. If the primal is **feasible**, how do we check to see if the primal is unbounded?

We can convert the primal LP to the dual LP. Then we can check to see if the dual LP is infeasible. If the dual LP is **infeasible**, then the primal LP is **either unbounded** or **infeasible**. However, we've already shown the primal LP to be feasible. Therefore, the primal LP must be **unbounded**.

1. Check if primal is feasible.
2. Check if dual is infeasible.

## Strong Duality
**Theorem:** Primal LP is feasible and bounded $\iff$ Dual LP is feasible and bounded.

> Primal has optimal point $x^*$ $\iff$ Dual has optimal point $y^*$.
> 
> $c^Tx^*=b^Ty^*$

If we write the LP for the max-flow problem, then the dual LP is the capacity of the min-cut problem. We can then verify that max-flow = min-cut using this strong duality theorem.

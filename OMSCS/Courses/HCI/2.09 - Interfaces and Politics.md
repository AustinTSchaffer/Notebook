# 2.09 - Interfaces and Politics

- Do technical devices have political implications?
- interfaces change the world around us
- sometimes change happens in ways we don't anticipate
- Designing for change
- anticipating change
- value-sensitive design
- some designs try to discourage certain behaviors
- 3 goals of HCI
	- Help
	- Understand
	- Change
## Winner, L: "Do Artifacts Have Politics?"
- inherently political technologies exist
- some technologies require top-down organization
- some technologies enable more distributed, egalitarian societies
- Someone named Moses intentionally constructed bridges that were too short for busses to prevent poor people from visiting his parks in Long Island, NY.
- Net neutrality

## Facebook
- Reaction options promote positivity
- Lack of a dislike option
- Added more "relationship" options beyond the original 5
	- civil union
	- domestic partnership
- Added more nuanced gender options

## Change by Happenstance
### Positive
- Pokemon Go
- Bicycles
	- Democratized movement
	- Enabled women to engage in the community on their own
	- Demanded a wardrobe change
### Negative
Wealthier areas with more developed infrastructure get better internet sooner. Perpetuates inequality.

## Value-Sensitive Design
- Privacy by design
- Distinguish between usability and human values
- Google removing "right to be forgotten" search links in Europe
- Conflict between cultures can make it impossible to develop unified global systems
- 5 tips
	- start early
	- know your users
	- consider direct and indirect stakeholders
	- brainstorm the interface's possibilities
	- choose carefully between supporting values and prescribing values
- How do we use interface design to promote positive change?
- What are the possible negative ramifications?

## Situated Action
- We must analyze our interfaces that we design in the context in which they'll be used
- We must understand the task the user performs grows out of interaction with the interface.
- The task doesn't exist until the user gets started, and once they start, they define the task.
- Memory is context-dependent.
- Recognition is easier than Recall.

## Activity Theory
- Where the concept "interacting through an interface" comes from
- Why is the user working the task in the first place?
	- Why are they doing it?
	- Required to?
	- Choose to?
- System of people and artifacts
- Actions can move up and down this diagram.
	- Conscious actions become subconscious operators after repetition/familiarity (ex: shifting gears in a car)
